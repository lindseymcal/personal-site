The study (home)

The study is the whole menu: every object is the one way into its part of the site, and it answers your pointer with its own small motion.

  • Bookshelf opens Books (one leaning book tips over). Desk and notebook open Writing (the lamp wobbles and a page lifts). Pegboard opens Projects (the tins sway).
  • Art site and Instagram, one route each: the framed painting opens your art site (it tilts on its nail), and a photo taped beside it, square like an Instagram post and tagged "art instagram", opens Instagram (it swings on its tape).
  • LinkedIn and résumé live in a small shoulder bag on the floor by the desk, an open-top bucket bag in dusty plum with a brass strap. The résumé stands in the opening at a low, casual angle and slides up and out along that angle on hover; LinkedIn is a round "in" charm on the strap that swings.
  • The only text always on screen is your name. Each object's tag appears on hover or keyboard focus, and each object has a screen-reader name.

The study on a phone

On a phone the study becomes one tall wall you scroll down, from the painting at the top to the shoulder bag on the floor, with small tags always showing because there is no hover.

I chose one tall wall over a grid of tiles (the key-frame version) because it keeps the room a room: you walk down it from the painting and photo, past the desk and lamp, the bookshelf and the pegboard, to the bag on the rug. Touch screens have no hover, so the tags stay visible, and a tap lights the object up before the page opens.

Books: the shelf, and a pulled-out book

A wall of real-looking books, one spine per book from your Goodreads shelf; pull one out and it comes forward with your rating, your review if you wrote one, and any note about who recommended it.

  • Shelves by year read, marked with small brass plates. A spine slides up when you point at it; the gap it leaves glows while it's out.
  • Your Goodreads rating (1 to 5) as five ink dots instead of stars. A book you read but didn't rate shows no dots at all.
  • With a review, the card is a letter. Without one, it's a small library card with just the rating and the date, and no "no review" text.
  • The date says "read Oct 2026", or "shelved Mar 2026" when Goodreads only has the date added. Same pencil, so it never looks like an error.
  • "Recommended by Ada" is a handwritten slip tucked into the book, shown only when you've added one.
  • Favorites get a gold-foil star stamped near the top of the spine, and the same star on the cover and beside the title when pulled out. You mark them in the same small file as "recommended by". Persuasion, Middlemarch, Moby-Dick, My Ántonia and Anna Karenina are starred here.
